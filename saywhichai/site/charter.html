AI transparency charter

Version 1.3, 19 November 2026

A short charter that a software project can adopt to say how AI tools are used in its development. It was first written for Vulnerability-Lookup.

The charter is about transparency, not judgement, and it is meant for everybody. It takes no side on the use of AI tools. A project that adopts it accepts contributions made with one, whatever your experience, including when you could not have written the change without it, and contributions made without one, whatever your reasons. What the project asks is that AI use is said, and that a person stands behind every contribution.

The commitments

  1. Say so. A commit that was not written by a human ends with a Co-Authored-By trailer naming the model and its version:

    Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>

    A pull request says in its description when a significant part of it was generated with an AI tool.

  2. A human answers for it. Every change is read, understood and tested by the person who submits it, whoever or whatever wrote it. That person states it in the pull request, in their own name. An AI tool never makes this statement for them.

  3. Be able to explain it. Reviewers may ask the author to explain any part of a change. The author answers themselves, not with pasted model output.

  4. A human submits. Pull requests, issues and vulnerability reports are opened by a person, after they have reviewed the work. An agent does not open them on its own.

  5. Report only what was reproduced. An issue or a vulnerability report describes a problem its author has observed, not an unverified finding of a tool.

  6. No shame, no ranking. Declaring the use of AI never counts against a contribution. The figures a project publishes about it describe what is declared; they are not a score.

  7. No one has to use AI. People who choose not to use AI tools are supported, not merely tolerated. The project never requires an AI tool to contribute: its documentation, its review and its help remain there for someone working without one. Their contributions are reviewed in the same way, and a maintainer who uses AI does not ask a contributor to do the same.

What "no AI declared" means

A commit without a trailer is a commit where no AI is declared. It may have been written entirely by hand, or the trailer may have been forgotten: nothing in a repository proves which. Anything published under this charter uses that wording and never says that someone "does not use AI".

Adopting the charter

A project adopts the charter by putting it into practice in four places:

FileWhat it carries
CONTRIBUTING.mdThe commitments above, in the project's own words, and the date they apply from
AGENTS.md (imported by CLAUDE.md and other agent files)The same rules, addressed to AI agents: add the trailer, never tick the human's box, never open a pull request alone
.github/PULL_REQUEST_TEMPLATE.mdTwo boxes, below
A project file in this repositorySo that the declared figures are published

The two boxes of the pull request template:

- [ ] Was a significant part of it generated with an AI tool? (If so, the commits carry a `Co-Authored-By` trailer naming the model.)
- [ ] I have read, understood and tested every change in this pull request, and I can explain it.

The first is a fact about the change. The second is the author's own statement, and only they tick it.

A project that adopts the charter says which version, for example in its CONTRIBUTING.md: "This project adopts the AI transparency charter 1.3." The good practice page has sections ready to copy.

What is published

For a project that adopts the charter, this repository builds a public page from the project's history: the models and versions named in commits, the people who contribute and the models each of them names, and, for each pull request, whether its boxes and the trailers of its commits tell the same story.

Only public data is read, and e-mail addresses are not published. Commits made before the project adopted the charter are shown for the history, and are not held to a rule that did not exist.

Versions

The charter is versioned so that a project knows what it adopted. A change of wording that keeps the commitments, or adds one, raises the second number; changing or removing a commitment raises the first, and projects choose when to move to it.
